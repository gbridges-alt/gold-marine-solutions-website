Common sanitation problems

If one of these is your boat, it's a job we do:

  • The toilet won't flush — nothing happens, or it flushes weakly and won't clear
  • The vacuum system won't hold — the vacuum drops away and the head won't operate properly
  • The vacuum pump keeps running — it cycles, or it won't cut out at all
  • The boat smells — and it still smells after a pump-out
  • The macerator isn't working — no discharge, or it runs without moving anything
  • Holding-tank problems — discharge, venting, or the tank not emptying properly
  • The level gauge is wrong — reading full when it isn't, or not reading at all
  • Leaks — at the pan, a hose, a fitting or the tank
  • Blockages — anywhere between the head and the discharge

You don't need to work out which of those it is before you call. Describe what the boat is doing and we'll take it from there.

How the system works, and what we cover

A marine sanitation system is a loop, not a fixture. The head, the flush-water supply, the vacuum generator or discharge pump, the hoses, the holding tank, its vent, the level sender, the macerator and the discharge valves all depend on each other — which is why a fault in one part can show up as a symptom somewhere else entirely.

A smell that won't clear after a pump-out can have a cause the pump-out was never going to address. A head that won't hold vacuum can be a seal, a valve, a hose or a switch. A tank reading full when it's empty can be telling you about the sender rather than the tank.

That's why the job starts with diagnosis rather than parts.

What we work on: VacuFlush systems, vacuum generators, toilet pumps, macerators, holding tanks, level switches and senders, valves, sanitation hoses, freshwater flush supply, and the associated 12V and 24V controls and switching — plus leaks, blockages, component replacement and full system diagnosis.

We work on VacuFlush and other marine sanitation systems. Gold Marine Solutions is not a Dometic dealer or authorised service centre.

It isn't always plumbing

Sanitation faults can also be control, switch or supply faults rather than purely plumbing faults.

A vacuum generator that won't cut out might be the switch telling it when to stop. A level sender reading wrong is an electrical fault on a plumbing system. A pump that does nothing at all might have nothing wrong with the pump — it might have no supply reaching it.

Because these systems combine plumbing with 12V or 24V controls, diagnosis needs to consider both sides from the start. Gold Marine checks the supply, switching and controls alongside the hoses, valves, tanks and pumps they operate.

That's the practical value of one contact: nobody has to decide which trade this is before the boat gets looked at.
