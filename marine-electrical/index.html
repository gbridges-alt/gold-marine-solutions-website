Not sure what's causing the problem?

You don't need to diagnose it before calling us.

A battery that keeps going flat can be the battery itself, a charging problem, an abnormal current draw, a connection issue or a switching fault. Those are different faults, and a new battery only addresses one of them — fit one without finding the cause and the original problem is still there.

So the job starts with testing. We measure what the batteries are actually doing, what the charging system is putting back, what the vessel draws with everything switched off, and whether current is getting where it should through the cables and switches in between.

Then you get told what the fault is, and what it takes to fix it. A no-crank can sit in the starting, charging or control circuit, or it can be mechanical — Gold Marine assesses both sides, so you don't have to pick the trade before you call.

Outboard — no-crank diagnosis and starter overhaul

Called out to a no-crank with a completely flat battery. A new battery on its own didn't fix it — testing found a shorted solenoid and high resistance through the starter. The starter was stripped, the brushes, terminals and armature cleaned, and the solenoid replaced.
