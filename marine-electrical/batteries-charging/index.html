We diagnose the system, not just the battery

A battery that keeps going flat can indicate a battery fault, charging problem, abnormal current draw, connection issue or switching fault.

Those are different fault paths, and a new battery only addresses the battery itself. Replacing it without identifying the underlying fault can leave the original problem unresolved.

So the job starts with testing. We measure what the batteries are actually doing, what the charging system is putting back, what the vessel is drawing when everything is switched off, and whether the current is getting where it should through the cables and switches in between.

Then you get told what the fault is, and what it takes to fix it.

What we test and repair

Batteries and banks

Battery condition testing, starting and house banks, replacement and correct fitment.

Charging systems

Charging voltage and output, alternators, rectifiers and charging-circuit faults.

Starting systems

Starter motors, starter circuits, solenoids and no-crank diagnosis.

Cabling, terminals and switching

Battery cables, terminals and lugs, isolators, switches and connections.

Voltage drop and current draw

Voltage-drop testing across the circuit, and abnormal or parasitic draw diagnosis when something is flattening the batteries overnight.

A no-crank isn't always electrical

A no-start can be mechanical or fuel related, or it can sit in the starting, charging or control circuit.

Gold Marine can assess both the mechanical and electrical side of the fault, so you don't need to work out which trade it is before you call. Describe what the engine is doing — nothing at all, a click, a slow crank, or turning over without firing — and we'll take it from there.
